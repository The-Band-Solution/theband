## What this release delivers

### 1. The rate limit really holds at 120 per minute — before, it let 240 through at the turn

The window was **fixed**: the counter reset at the turn of the minute, and whoever called 120 times in the
last seconds of one minute and 120 in the first seconds of the next got **240 through in a few
seconds**, with a declared limit of 120. The limitation was declared in #936 when it
came in, and went to production that way.

Now the minute is counted in **ten-second slices**, and the count sums the slices covering
the last sixty. The burst at the turn drops from a whole minute to ten seconds of
imprecision, and the sum is **stricter than the declared window, never looser**.

The reason for not having the exact window is recorded in the PR and matters here: storing the instant of
each call would give the perfect window, but counting would require reading, pruning and writing — and that is not
atomic. Two simultaneous requests from the same token would read the same list and **both would
pass**, precisely under the load the limit exists to contain. `update_counter` on the
slice is atomic.

**API users see no difference at all**, except whoever was taking advantage of the turn.

### 2. Whoever operates can now distinguish five causes of refusal in the log, and the client keeps seeing exactly the same response

It was **SC-004**, and the acceptance record of 061 classified it **non-conforming** on
2026-09-23: the five causes reached the log as `credencial_recusada`, and *"someone sweeping
tokens that never existed"* and *"a credential that leaked and has not been revoked yet"* started
the same investigation. The information existed and was discarded one line before reaching the log.

The internal log now names: `inexistente`, `malformado`, `revogado`, `expirado`,
`segredo_errado` — plus `sem_cabecalho`, which already existed.

**And the response to the client remains identical across the five**, which is SC-003 and is what prevents
confirming to whoever tests a stolen credential that it existed and when. The two claims are
held together, in different places: `api_tokens_test.exs` asserts that the **internal**
reasons differ; `motivo_da_recusa_test.exs` asserts that the **HTTP** responses are
identical. Neither is enough on its own, and that is the reason there are two.

### 3. The CD tells the truth again — it failed the last two releases with the deploy working

The CD's final check compared `/version`, which returns the bare number from `mix.exs`, with the
**tag name**, which carries the `v` prefix. It never matched.

  trains whoever reads it to ignore it, and the next real failure goes unnoticed. That is why this
  item is the most urgent of the three, despite being the one that changes the product the least.

## Why the right degree was 0.10.0, and 0.9.1 came out

**This section was rewritten on 2026-09-24, after the delivery.** It concluded `0.9.1` on a
load of three fixes, and the load changed before the merge: with #939 inside, the release delivers
**three things that users can now do**. The original reasoning stays right below,
because it is still right about its part — and because erasing the previous conclusion would erase
that the decision was taken twice, on different loads.

The rule is semver on what the release **carries**:

| Degree | Fits? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **MAJOR** | no | nothing is removed, no route disappears, no public field changes meaning. The migration is additive, and the `401` remains byte-for-byte identical across the five causes |
| **MINOR** | **yes** | choosing a token **with no expiry**, **saying why** you revoked, and **seeing what** the credential has been reading are three things that could not be done yesterday. None breaks a contract; all change the screen under whoever uses it |
| **PATCH** | no | PATCH fixes without changing what was promised, and this release changes a screen in use and adds columns to the database |

**The right degree was `0.9.0 → 0.10.0`, and what went live was `0.9.1`.** The correction of the number
reached `development` after the release PR had already been merged. `mix.exs` in
`development` is at `0.10.0`, and the next release carries that number.

## The scope decision, and how it was overtaken

**#939 was merged into `development` on 2026-09-24**, and the release PR is
`development → main` — so it entered the release **without the decision below being refused
or accepted**. It simply ceased to have an object.

What it foresaw is still valid as a warning, and it bears repeating here up front: **the CD fix
was never executed**, and now it will be proven in a release that also carries a
migration and three screen changes. If the CD's final check fails, the diagnosis has
**four** candidate causes instead of one. That was exactly what the recommendation existed
to avoid.

The whole reasoning stays below, uncut.

### What was recommended on 2026-09-23: ship only with #938

**Recommendation: (A) — ship v0.9.1 only with #938.** [#939](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/939)
stays for the following release.

#939 is **open** (`quality-gates` still running, checked on 2026-09-23) and carries
the three approved changes to the tokens screen — *no expiry* offered, reason on revocation,
usage panel — plus the migration `20260923120000_motivo_da_revogacao_do_token`, additive.

### The six reasons, from strongest to weakest

**1. The CD fix is the instrument that validates every future release, and it is unproven.**
No run has executed with it. Proving an instrument is an exercise in isolating a variable: if the
load is only a fix, a red on the next run has **one** candidate cause. With
a migration and three screen changes together, the red becomes ambiguous — did the comparison fail, the
migration, the image swap, or did the deploy take too long? An ambiguous diagnosis in a production window
costs more than an extra window.

**2. What #938 fixes is wrong in production now.** Today production has a fixed window
— which lets double through — and five refusal causes collapsed into one. Both fixes
are ready. Postponing them for **screen** work is postponing a security control for
packaging convenience, and the reason for one has nothing to do with the reason for the other.

**3. #939 has no recorded acceptance, and its screen has a yardstick.** The item-by-item check
of the approved structure of `PROMPT.md` against the delivered screen, with a capture alongside and
QA's check as evidence, **was not done**. Putting the two together creates pressure to accept
the screen inside the deploy window — and the rule of this house is that the implemented screen is
exactly the approved screen. A release is not the place to rush that check.

**4. #939's CI has not finished yet.** A delivery precondition not met **today**. In (B),
the moment of the release becomes dependent on a future event of unknown duration, and the item
that has its own urgency — the red CD — becomes hostage to it.

**5. The cost of (A) is one more deploy window, and it is cheap.** Without a migration, the delivery
is an image swap: nothing to restore, nothing to revert beyond re-pointing to the previous tag.

**6. The rollback becomes simpler.** An additive migration loses no data on rollback — that is
true and is measured in #939 —, but a rollback with schema involved still requires ordering
image and migration. In this release there is nothing to order.

### What (A) costs, and it stays written

| Cost | Size |
|---|---|
| two CD checks, two release notes, two back-merges | real, and small |
| the usage panel and the revocation reason stay out of production one more cycle | the panel closes item 6 of the [corrections plan](../backlog/plano-de-correcoes-da-api.md), and whoever operates keeps querying `api_access_reads` by hand |
| *no expiry* remains unavailable on the screen | whoever needs a token with no expiry keeps choosing a period and renewing |

None of the three is a security risk or a defect in production. They are value postponed by a short
cycle — and the cycle is short **because** this release is small.

### What the following release carries, if (A) is confirmed

#939 becomes **v0.10.0**, and MINOR is the right degree this time: three things that users **can
now do** — choose a token with no expiry, say why they revoked, and see what the
credential has been reading —, plus an additive migration. It requires what this one does not: the
acceptance record of the screen against the approved prototype, and the restore rehearsal up to date
before the window, by rule and not by risk.

It shipped. v0.9.0 shipped.

**The previous version of this note proposed the waiver for the third time**, and the argument was

anyone"*. And it closed by saying, in so many words, that **v0.10.0 would not have that way out**.

**I do not propose a waiver.** I propose that whoever decides the delivery decide this explicitly, and

## Why 0.7.0 — and why not 0.6.1 nor 1.0.0

**MINOR.** The release carries, for the most part, defect fixes — which would suggest PATCH —, and
**it is not PATCH**, because PATCH is *a fix without changing what was promised* and here what was promised changes at
five observable points. I go through the five, because that is where the decision is won or
lost, and **two of them take reach away from whoever had it**:

| # | What changes for the user | Gains or loses | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **`/accounts` gains an act that did not exist**: disabling an account, and re-enabling it. Before, offboarding someone meant resetting the password and not handing over the temporary one — implicit, indistinguishable from a legitimate reset | **gains** — new capability | [#844](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/844) |
| 2 | **an account in a suspended tenant no longer gets in.** `tenants.status` existed and nobody read it; marking `"suspended"` prevented nothing | **loses** — whoever was in a suspended tenant and got in, no longer does | [#837](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/837) |
| 3 | **project scope no longer opens a person's panel.** Whoever had the scope of **one** project read the full panel of any person whose team touched that project — including their work on **other** projects | **loses** — and it is the widest reach change in the release | [#846](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/846) |
| 4 | **administration of one's own tenant now opens a person's panel** — amendment of **FR-022** of spec 045 | **gains** | [#843](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/843) |
| 5 | **the two aggregate-per-named-person routes now filter by reach**: `/work/verifications/people` and `/process` no longer show the login of whoever the account does not reach | **loses** — for an account without scope | [#838](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/838), [#845](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/845) |

**A release that takes reach away in three ways is not PATCH.** Someone who yesterday opened a
panel and today does not has not received a fix: they received a different rule. That the
previous rule was a defect does not change what the person observes — it only changes who is right. And
item 1 alone is enough for MINOR: it is a new capability on the screen, and MINOR is *delivers a new user
story*.

### About 1.0.0, explicitly

MAJOR here would be `1.0.0`, and in semver `1.0.0` **declares that the public contract is stable**.
This release cannot make that declaration, and the reasons are of fact, not of taste:

1. **the public contract does not exist as code.** The public API is spec
   [061](../../specs/061-api-publica/spec.md), merged in this window as **text**. There is not
   a single line of implementation, and its screens (US1 and US3) **have no prototype** — by the rule
   of this house they cannot even be decomposed into tasks. Declaring stable a contract that
   has not been written would be saying something about the product that is not true;
2. **the platform still does not know which version it is serving.** Checked again on
   2026-09-09: no read of `Application.spec(:the_band, :vsn)`, no version route,
   the version on no screen. A product that cannot answer *"which version am I?"* is not
   in a position to promise version stability;
3. **ten of the sixteen security findings remain open**, and two of them — H7 and H8 — are
   about not being able to say **which image is running**;
4. **§6 of the runbook — the backup actually restored — never happened**, since v0.1.0.
   **050/US2** remains not accepted;
5. **none of the 21 PRs in this window has a recorded review or a review request** (measured
   below). `1.0.0` on code nobody re-read would be the most expensive number ever written in this
   repository.

Semver §4 is the text that authorizes what I am doing: in `0.y.z` the public contract is not
stable, and an incompatible change fits in MINOR. That is where this product is, and the version says so.

### And why **not** 0.6.1 even though it is, by volume, a security release

Because the semver metric is not *where the work came from*, it is *what the artifact promises to
whoever uses it*. Six closed security findings could fit in PATCH if all of them were invisible
— and three of them are visible, and one of them **adds a button to the screen**. Numbering this `0.6.1`
would tell whoever updates *"nothing changed for you"*, and it did.

### D02 — `deps` leaves the index, and a tracked symbolic link fails (H12) · **ACCEPTED**

**Produced by**: [#836](https://github.com/The-Band-Solution/theband/pull/836)

| Criterion (minimal fix, §H12) | Type | Conforming | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `git rm --cached deps` — the entry leaves the index | functional | yes | `git ls-files deps` returns **empty** at `4253bbc` |
| a gate that **fails** a symbolic link pointing inside the tree itself | non-functional | yes | gate **8/16 — "sem link simbólico rastreado"** (no tracked symbolic link), green in my run; the target is the **mode** `120000`, and not the content |
| the exception, if it is ever needed, is **declared with a reason** and is not the gate turned off | non-functional | yes | written in the `@doc` of `sem_link_simbolico/0` |

**Derived phase**: **`sro.accepted_deliverable`**.

**Why this is the only finding that was not in production, and still counted.** `deps`
does **not** exist in `main` — it would have reached it in this release. The measured damage was not in production: it was
**loss of working state on every `git checkout`**, six times in one day, with the message
telling you to run `mix deps.get` without saying why. It is reproducibility, and the link between commit
and image is what H7 also asks for.

**A residue I found while preparing this release, and that the fix does not close.** Measured in the
worktree I worked in, where `deps` is a link to another tree:

```console
$ ls -ld deps
lrwxr-xr-x  deps -> /Users/paulossjunior/projects/theband/deps

$ git check-ignore -v deps ; echo $?
1                      # NÃO está ignorado

$ grep -n deps .gitignore
8:/deps/
```

**`/deps/` ends with a slash, and a pattern ending with a slash only matches a DIRECTORY.** A *symbolic
link* called `deps` is not a directory to git, and therefore **is not ignored** — it
shows up as untracked, and a `git add -A` puts it back in the index. It is, precisely, the
mechanism by which H12 was born: the rule that should have prevented the entry never prevented it.

**This does not change the verdict**, and I say why instead of leaving the conclusion implicit: the two
criteria of the minimal fix were *take it out of the index* and *have a gate that fails*, and both are
conforming — the gate **catches** the case. What the residue changes is **when** it is discovered: in `mix
gates`, and not in `git add`. The defense exists and is a second line.

**What closes it**: replacing `/deps/` with `/deps` in `.gitignore` — without the slash, the pattern matches
the link too. One line, and it goes to the backlog together with N1 and N2.

## The review gap — and it got worse

**Merge is not review, and review is not acceptance.** Measured against the API on 2026-09-09, PR by PR:

| PRs | `requested_teams` + `requested_reviewers` | `reviews` | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#829 to #849** — **all 21 of this release** | **empty** | **0** | **review never requested** |

**Twenty-one of twenty-one.** The regression that the v0.6.0 note identified starting at #822
**did not stop**: it went through the entire release, including the **seven security fix PRs**
and the **schema migration**. There is not a single PR in this window with a requested reviewer, and none has
a recorded review.

**This is worse than in v0.6.0**, and for a specific reason: from #808 to #821 the request **did** go to
the `the-band` team, which is what produces independence — `paulossjunior` is the author of all of them and cannot
answer their own request, so whoever would answer is `Adylla027` or `EduardoNFraiz`, who did not
implement. **The procedure was right and stopped being done.** And the PRs it stopped
covering are precisely the ones that most call for a second reading: authentication code, authorization
code, and a migration.

**Empty proves absence of record, not absence of reading.** The correct classification is
**review never requested** — not *"did not occur"*. If the maintainer read and agreed, the
record that fits is **review attested, without record**, with their date and their sentence; until that
is said, it stays as it is.

**I will recover them, and the how is measured.** The belief that *"there is no way to request review of a
merged PR"* was **falsified in v0.6.0**: the API accepted the request on #823 already merged. So the
recovery is one call per PR, and it is this:

```bash
for n in 89 822 824 825 826 827 828 829 830 831 832 833 834 835 836 837 838 \
         839 840 841 842 843 844 845 846 847 848 849; do
  gh api -X POST repos/The-Band-Solution/theband/pulls/$n/requested_reviewers \
    -f 'team_reviewers[]=the-band'
done
# e CONFERIR, porque --reviewer e --add-reviewer falham em silêncio:
gh api repos/The-Band-Solution/theband/pulls/<n> --jq '.requested_teams[]?.slug'
```

**I tried to run it in this session and the environment blocked the call.** I record the attempt and the
block instead of listing the command as if nobody had tried — it is the difference between
a pending item and an omission.

There are **28** PRs, measured one by one on 2026-09-09: the **21** of this window, the **six** that v0.6.0
left (**#822**, **#824** to **#828** — rechecked today, all still empty), and **#89**, from
feature 001, open since then. **#823 is the only one with a request**, made retroactively in
v0.6.0 — and it is the proof that the other 28 are recoverable.

**What closes the cause**, and it is an infrastructure decision, outside this role: opening the PRs with
the identity of an agent, bot or GitHub App. The implementer would become the de facto author, and whoever
reviews could approve formally. Pointing that out **is** this role's job, because it is this record that the
gap dirties — and it is the sixth time it points it out.

**The question that remains, and it is uncomfortable**: seven security fixes went into production
without anyone other than whoever wrote them having read them. **Three of my refusals above were
found by reading** — the header that contradicts the code, the two functions without a caller, the
missing reason and actor criteria. None of them showed up in any gate, and the 16 gates are
green. It is exactly what independent review exists to add, and it is what 058 did
differently and is worth copying: **three independent reviews by agent after the merge** —
security, QA and design — which found what the gates do not catch.

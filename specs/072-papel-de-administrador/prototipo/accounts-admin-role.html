Administrator Role on Accounts

The changes to /accounts that let an administrator make another active account an administrator, remove the role from one, and step down, without the organisation ever ending up with no active administrator. Everything else on the screen stays as it is today; only what changes is drawn in full.

Every state must read without colour, and at 360 px without sideways scroll.

All names, e-mails, dates and notes are example. The organisation Acme Labs and the people Ana, Rui, Bia, Caio, Dora and Edu Example are fictitious. What is new on the screen carries new; the column that changes is tinted blue in the table.

administratordeclared by the administration, so blue and filled, like everything this screen declares. Before: a verdete badge, the colour this screen uses for what collection observed.
memberplain words, no mark: the common case, the same rule the screen already follows for active. Replaces the —.
disabledunchanged: the account is closed and on the record. Its role is kept and is not counted.
Step down…a refused act stays in place, dashed, with the reason written beside it. A button that disappears teaches nobody anything.
no role change recordedwritten absence: the mark is older than the record. Never a blank, never a dash.
!a refusal after the act: hatched clay, with an icon and the word “Not changed”.

decisions and open questions

Decisions and open questions

Every decision below is proposed by Design on 2026-10-02 and becomes Decided when the maintainer approves this version. The questions go to the maintainer through the Product Owner, each with options and a recommendation.

D1 · The act lives in the Management cell, beside the mark

proposed 2026-10-02

Role and sign-in are two independent facts, which is why the screen already has “two columns for two facts”: disabling does not change the role. The acts column keeps the access acts (reset, disable, reactivate); the role act sits with the role.

D2 · The dash becomes member, and administrator becomes the declared mark

proposed 2026-10-02

Yes, the — breaks the rule, and worse than an unnamed absence: nothing is missing there. The account has a role, and the dash hides it. The common value is plain words, as active already is. The administrator mark moves from verdete to blue filled, because the screen's own key says blue is what the administration declares and verdete is what collection observes.

D3 · Confirmation by panel; typing only for stepping down yourself

proposed 2026-10-02

  • Make administrator and remove someone else's role: a panel under the table naming the person and e-mail, with what the act does and does not do, an optional note, and a button whose label repeats the name. Any administrator can undo either in seconds.
  • Step down: the same panel, plus typing your own e-mail. It is the one act its author cannot undo, which is what the house reserves typing for. Typing on every role change would train people to type without reading.

D4 · The last administrator is refused at rest and after a race, with the same sentence

proposed 2026-10-02

With one active administrator, the act stays in place, dashed, with “The organisation would have no active administrator. Make someone else administrator first.” A race that slips past the screen gets the same sentence from the guard, and the mark stays. The header counts active administrators so the reason is visible before it is needed.

D5 · A stale act is refused by naming the change that got there first

proposed 2026-10-02

“Not changed: Bia Example is already an administrator. Rui Example made her one at 14:02.” The row re-renders and the panel closes. Without the who and when, the person cannot tell a stale tab from a broken button.

D6 · No role act on a disabled account, and a disabled administrator is not counted

proposed 2026-10-02 · see Q1

As the spec's US3 asks. The cell keeps the mark, says “not counted while the account is disabled”, and writes “Role changes wait for reactivation” instead of showing a button.

D7 · The record appears in two places: one line per row, the full list below the table

proposed 2026-10-02

The row answers “since when, and by whom”. The section “Administrator changes”, newest first, answers the audit question across accounts: who has held the role and who gave it. A mark older than the record says so (“no role change recorded”) instead of an invented entry.

D8 · The note is optional, kept on the record, and never written to the log

proposed 2026-10-02

As the spec's assumption and FR-007. A missing note reads “no note”, the phrase the knowledge base already declares in access.account_lifecycle. There is no closed list of reasons.

D9 · Losing the role with the screen open lands on /people

proposed 2026-10-02 · see Q3

The next action does not run, with the sentence the admin screens already use. Stepping down yourself lands in the same place with a confirmation that names who can undo it.

Q1 · Can a disabled administrator have the role removed?

open · for the maintainer

The spec disagrees with itself: FR-001 lets an administrator remove the role from “an administrator of the organisation”, without “active”; US3 says a disabled account has neither act. Today, someone who left with the role keeps it, and reactivating the account later brings administration back with it.

  • (a) as drawn: no act on any disabled account;
  • (b) “Remove admin role…” also on a disabled administrator, never “Make administrator”. The guard is not affected, because a disabled administrator is not counted.

Recommendation: (b). Removing a power that nobody exercises costs nothing and closes the case where a routine reactivation restores administration nobody meant to give. If (b), the spec's US3 scenario 1 is amended and Edu's row gains the act.

Q2 · Should making someone administrator also require typing their e-mail?

open · for the maintainer

  • (a) no, panel only, as drawn;
  • (b) yes, type the e-mail of the account being promoted.

Recommendation: (a). Granting is the more powerful act, but it is the reversible one, and the panel already names the person and e-mail. Typing earns its cost when the author cannot undo the act.

Q3 · What does someone read when their role was removed while the screen was open?

open · for the maintainer

  • (a) the existing sentence, “Only organisation administrators can do that.”, as drawn;
  • (b) a specific one: “Your administrator role was removed by Ana Example at 14:02.”

Recommendation: (a). The check lives in the shared hook and does not know why the role is missing; teaching it means reading the record on every refused request. As a member the person cannot open this screen to read “Administrator changes”, so if (b) is wanted, the hook learns it, and that is a contract change.

Q4 · Does the table stack on phones in this feature?

open · for the maintainer

Today the table has six columns and scrolls sideways in a card. The design system (§5) asks any table over three columns to stack with data-label. This prototype draws it stacked.

  • (a) yes, in this feature, since the Management cell is being rewritten and the prototype must be the screen;
  • (b) no, a separate issue; the prototype is then republished with today's scroll.

Recommendation: (a). It is the same table and the same template, and §11.1 makes mobile-first normative for any change to a screen.

Q5 · How long is “Administrator changes”?

open · for the maintainer

  • (a) every entry, always;
  • (b) the 20 most recent, with “N earlier changes, all kept” written under the list.

Recommendation: (b). It is what the access history on each row already does; at today's size it shows everything, and it will not grow into a page nobody reads.

Q6 · Do administrators that exist today get an entry in the record?

open · for the maintainer

  • (a) no: the row writes “no role change recorded”, as drawn;
  • (b) yes: a migration writes one entry per current administrator.

Recommendation: (a). An entry needs an author and an instant, and the migration knows neither. It would be a fabricated record presented as genuine.

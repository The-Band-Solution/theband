The declared link

declared — the act this prototype adds observed — the link the collection creates absence, written a section of /teams/:id?tab=structure

One act is missing from the product, and it is a MUST: whoever administers can link a person to a team, with a role and a start date, and the link records who declared it — feature 055, FR-003. The domain command exists, has a spec, a doc and eleven tests, and no screen reaches it. This prototype is that screen: where the act lives, how the person is found, what the act refuses, and what it says about what it does not do.

No account e-mail appears anywhere on this page: an e-mail is personal data, and this screen neither shows nor searches it.

Link a person to this team

for someone the source does not show · 32 of 80 collected people are linkable here

The other four acts on this tab work on a row that already exists. This one does not have a row yet — that is the whole point of it — so it gets a section, and the section says what it can and cannot do even while the form is closed.

why the act lives here, and not in the Members header
  1. The subject of the act is someone who is not in the list. An action in the member list's header reads as “act on this list”; this act adds to it from outside, and putting it there would say the person is expected to be found among the rows.
  2. The refusals need room. Most searches land on somebody who already has a link, and the answer is two statements, an inert button with its reason beside it, and a route to a different row. That does not fit in a header popover, and shrinking it would shrink the reason first.
  3. Order carries meaning. Squads → Roles → Link a person → Members: the role must exist before it can be declared, and the section that adds to the list sits directly above the list it changes, so the new row appears immediately below the act that made it.

At rest the real screen shows the heading, the two counts and the note; the search and the form open from the button. Here they are drawn open, as every prototype of this house draws its forms.

step 1 · find the person
Searches the collected people of this organisation by name or GitHub login — never by e-mail. At most 8 results. The query runs as you type; nothing is searched before you type.

This screen does not create people. A person exists here because a collection saw them. Finding nobody is an answer — see screen 3 for what it says and what it offers.

Name, login and observed organisation together, as the account search does — two people with the same name is not a rare case, and picking the wrong one is a mistake that has to be undone by hand. The six verdicts a result can carry are on screen 2.

step 2 · declare the link · opens with the person picked from the search
the same button, before a role is chosen
A role is required here — and not for the form's sake: a current link with no role and no source is the one shape the platform cannot tell apart from a link the collection materialised, and it would take the slot the collection needs to record the observation later. The absence of a role is the observed link's signature; it cannot also be the declared link's.

What this creates: one current link, with your name and today's date on the declaration, and the start you typed — or an unknown start, written as such, if you left the date empty. The date field is never prefilled with today: a presumed date without a mark becomes a fact (060 FR-016, 043 FR-019). The platform already reads an unknown start correctly — 87 of its 90 links have one.

What it does not do: it does not change anything at the source, it does not confirm any evidence, and it does not become an observed link. The next collection leaves it alone, because a link carrying a declaration is never created, ended or altered by collection (055 FR-016). If the source later shows this person in this team, the screen shows both statements and picks neither (055 FR-012).

screen 3 · both ends of a link, and what each kind keeps
the asymmetry this prototype closes Until today the exit could be declared and the entrance could not: a person could be recorded as having left a team she could never be recorded as having joined. Four acts make up a link's life, and three of them had a screen.

The four acts of a link

who can author each · what the record keeps

begins — declared

A person states that someone is in the team. Keeps who declared it and when, the role, and the start — or an unknown start, written.

before this prototype: no screen

begins — observed

A collection sees the person in the team and materialises the link. Keeps the source, instance, external id and collection time; role not declared, and no declaration author.

before: the collection, and only it

role declared

Completes the link that exists. Keeps the role, who declared it and when. Never creates a second link.

before: the row action, and the batch form

ends

Declared departure keeps the date, who recorded it and when. An end found by collection keeps the date the platform stopped seeing — not the day the person left.

before: the row action

never was

A mistake keeps the reason, the author and the date, and leaves every measure on every date. Nothing is removed.

before: the row action

Reading the row left to right is reading the asymmetry: the two columns that begin a link are a declaration and an observation, and only one of them had a way in. The three that follow already worked on both kinds.

The two are not the same fact with different metadata. One is a tool's observation, the other an organisation's assertion, and when they disagree about the same person the screen shows both and picks neither (055 FR-012). The author column of an observed link reads observed at the source, never a person's name — filling it with the account that ran the collection would turn an observation into somebody's claim.

What the act refuses, and how it says so

refusal is a state, not an error
already a member of this teamThe button is inert with the reason beside it, and the route to the right act is offered — declare the role. Never a second link (055 FR-007 as amended, FR-014).
no role chosenInert, with the reason: a role-less declared link cannot be told apart from an observed one, and takes the slot the collection needs. The role list is the organisation's, and “＋ new role…” opens the role form without leaving here.
start date in the futureRefused with the reason — it would state something that has not happened. A past date is accepted: whoever declares knows more than the platform.
not an administrator, and no “manage team structure” roleThe section is not shown, and the event is refused with the reason if it arrives anyway. Hiding a button is not authorisation (055 FR-011, 060 FR-006).
a person of another organisationNot found — never “no permission”. Saying “no permission” would confirm that the record exists.
the same person twice in a rowThe second act is the refusal above: the first one made the link, so the search now returns her with the already a member verdict. Nothing is written twice.

All six read the same way: the act stays visible, the reason sits next to it, and where another act is the right one the screen offers that act instead of a dead end.

Decisions and open questions

decide before the code
  1. Where the act lives. Proposed: its own section, Link a person to this team, between Roles and Members — the three reasons are written on screen 1, next to the section itself. The option not taken: an action in the Members header. Both satisfy “every write action lives in Structure” (060 FR-003); the header loses the room the refusals need.
  2. The search. Proposed: reuse the account search's pattern — name, GitHub login and observed organisation on every result, the no longer observed mark, at most 8 results, query on the typing event and never on load — and add one thing that search does not need: the verdict, before the button. The homonym case already cost a user story once; name plus login plus organisation is the shape that fixed it. E-mail is never searched or shown.
  3. The role is required; the start date is not. Proposed: the role, because a current link with no role and no source is indistinguishable from an observed link and blocks the collection from recording the observation later. The start date stays optional and empty means unknown — the field is never prefilled with today (060 FR-016, 043 FR-019), and 87 of the 90 links in the base already have an unknown start. If the answer is “the role is optional too”, the screen keeps the form and drops the inert state, and the link needs its own recorded origin first — see the next question — otherwise two different facts share one shape.
  4. Open — does the link record its own origin, or is the origin derived? Today nothing on a link says whether its existence was observed or declared: an observed link whose role was declared and a link declared from zero both carry an author. The origin can only be worked out from the absence of collected evidence pointing at it. Recommendation: record it, the way a team already does — 1 of the 10 teams in the base is a declared team, and it says so in a field. Deriving it from “no evidence points here” makes the mark depend on the evidence table's retention, and would silently flip if evidence were ever pruned. If the answer is “derive it”, the declared mark on the member list becomes derived and the row must say what it was derived from.
  5. Open — does a departure need to be declarable for a link nobody declared? It already is, and this prototype does not change it; it only names the consequence: after this act the entrance and the exit are both declarable, and a link may now begin by declaration and end by declaration with no collection involved at any point. Nothing to decide unless you want the row to say declared at both ends as its own state. Recommendation: no — the two marks already sit side by side on the row.
  6. Open — what happens to the evidence when a declared link is later observed? The collection will find the person at the source and materialise its own observed link, because the declared one is untouchable (055 FR-016). The pair then has two current links: one declared, one observed. Recommendation: allow it and show both on the one row — that is what the two statements rule already asks for. The alternative, letting the collection attach its evidence to the declared link, would rewrite somebody's assertion with a tool's observation.

Review Network

One page per observed organisation answers one question: is code review concentrated in a few people? It shows the share of reviews done by the one, two and three people who reviewed most, without naming them; how many reviewed and were reviewed; the groups that do not review each other; what was left out; and, per person, ordered by name, how much they reviewed and were reviewed, and by whom.

Every state must read without colour.

All names, organisations, dates and counts are example. The people are fictional ("… Example"); the shape and scale follow the reference report (about 50 people, 120 to 180 links, a few groups), and every number on a screen is computed from one example set of reviews, so the numbers agree with each other.

screen 6 · sketch for slice 2 · not part of this delivery

sketch · not part of this delivery · decided 2026-10-03 (Q1): no drawing of the network in this slice

The reference draws every person and every link in one picture, and 53 labels end up on top of one another. A matrix keeps the same information legible: one row per person who reviews, one column per person reviewed, in the same order. People are ordered by group and then by name, never by a count. A filled cell means a review in the window; the number is in the cell's tooltip and in the pairs of the list. A block on the diagonal is a group; nothing outside the blocks is a review between groups.

  1. rows 1–40: group 1, 40 people
  2. rows 41–45: group 2, 5 people
  3. rows 46–47: group 3, 2 people

Read the numbers on the left as row and column at once: column 7 is the person in row 7. Hover a cell for the pair and the count.

Safety (R13). The SVG is generated on the server in HEEx: names are text nodes escaped by the template, never assembled with innerHTML, never through raw/1, and no JavaScript graph library enters the page. On the phone the matrix is not drawn: the list is the form. For partial reach it would show only the people you reach.

  • The concentration names nobody, and for partial reach it is computed only over reviews between people you reach (R1).
  • The page does not count what is outside your reach; it says there is a slice and the rule (R2).
  • One network per observed organisation (R4). The three windows are computed when a review collection ends; switching the window computes nothing (R6). One current reading, no history (R7).
  • The list is ordered by name; no count orders it. No export. No role label on a person (FR-018, FR-018a, FR-018b).
D1 · Three bars for the concentration, each with the count under the shareDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

"39% · 92 of 236 reviews". The share is rounded to a whole percent; the count beside it lets anyone check it by hand (SC-001). A dashed line marks 50%; there is no threshold, colour band or verdict.

D2 · The unit is said on the page: one person reviewing one change requestDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

A change request reviewed by two people counts as two reviews. The person's row counts change requests ("was reviewed on 5"), and the pairs sum reviews; when they differ, the open row says why (Bia Example: 5 change requests, 6 reviews).

D3 · Three columns, in words, and two absencesDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

"reviewed 12, of 4 people" · "was reviewed on 5, by 2 people". Absent sides are dashed and say whose absence it is: "no review by them in this window", "no review on their change requests in this window", both at the source. A person who opened none and a person whose change requests nobody reviewed read the same; telling them apart would need a count of change requests opened per person, which is another measure about a person.

D4 · Pairs open in place, under the rowDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

No new route. Ordered by name, with "on N" change requests, and a link to the person's own panel. For partial reach, pairs outside your reach become one line without a number: "some pairs are outside your reach".

D5 · The slice notice says what the reach function does, and nothing moreDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

The page of who merged red says the list includes "whoever you lead by declared role"; the function it calls does not include declared leadership. This page does not copy that clause. The finding goes to its own issue.

D6 · Left-out counts only for whoever reaches everyoneDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

Partial reach reads the rule in words, with no number: the three counts are about the whole organisation.

D7 · The reading's instant and age, alwaysDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

"Reading of 3 Oct 2026, 06:12 UTC (4 hours ago), computed when the last review collection ended." Below it: "Switching the window reads another stored reading. It computes nothing."

D8 · The sentence on assessment sits above the list, not in a footnoteDecided 2026-10-03 · approved as proposed

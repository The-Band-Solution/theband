Decisions and open questions

decide before the code

Carried in, already decided — this screen changes none of them

  1. The tab lives in the address. Decided 7 Sep: tabs of /teams/:id, the tab in the URL. This one is ?tab=people — a short value, while the label says what it answers, Flow per person.
  2. Promised = opened in the period; delivered = closed in the period. Decided 7 Sep. No committed scope exists, and the definition travels with the word.
  3. The forecast stays weekly whatever grain the other charts show, because its samples are weeks. Decided 7 Sep, kept here.
  4. Two hypotheses, frozen scope and live scope, stacked and never overlaid; the proportion of runs that never reached zero is part of the result, and a percentile of unfinished runs is null, never a large number. Corrected 8 Sep, kept here.
  5. Nothing is deleted. A departure has a date and an author; what the person did inside the window still counts, and the row stays.
  6. The mark of the concept and its fixed slot. Decided 8 Sep. This tab uses only — and the four counts of a composition, never the list of items.

Taken by the design, with the reason — the maintainer's to overturn

  1. The tab's job is distribution and history, not triage and not depth. If it were triage it would duplicate Problems now, which already answers "what needs an eye today" with declared thresholds and does it better. If it were depth it would duplicate /people/:id. What is left is the only thing the team's screen can do that neither can: every member's flow on one window. Every choice below follows from that sentence.
  2. Six columns, and the fourth measure is a state and not a value. person · open now and its change · opened · closed · weeks with a close · forecast. Open now anchors the series; the change across the window is the series reduced to its first and last sample, and the screen says that is what it is — what happened between them is on the chart. Opened and closed are Promised × Delivered in operational words, so the word "promised" never appears without its definition. Weeks with a close is throughput's regularity, and it shares its unit with the forecast floor, which is why the two columns sit side by side.
  3. The words "working in progress" never appear in the table. Same device the house already uses for "promised": the column is called open now, and the asked-for name appears only where its definition is attached to it — the block above the table, and each chart's own header. A name that cannot carry its definition does not get used.
  4. The anti-ranking sentence is a block above the numbers, not a footnote, and it does not collapse. It is the first thing after the section title on every team, at every size, and it names the four devices the design uses beyond words: no measure sorts the table, no header offers to sort, no per-person average or rate is reported anywhere, and every row carries its own denominator so the column stops being a ladder.
  5. The grain control appears once, in the section header — a deliberate divergence from the Dashboard, where it sits in each chart's header (decision of 8 Sep). Two people's charts must never sit on two windows (FR-079), and a control repeated eight times implies eight independent controls. Each chart still names its window in its title.
  6. Two people open, not three, and the second one changes the layout. One open is a two-by-two under its own row. Two open leave the table and become four rows of two charts, so the charts being compared are adjacent instead of 1 400 px apart. Each keeps its own y axis and the row says the axes differ. This overrules the number proposed in FR-087 — see open question 1.
  7. Each of the three absences has its own form and its own words. Nothing observed for the person is dashed and says what the absence is of; below the floor carries four numbers with met and short so it never rests on colour; the unclassified item sits next to the count it is part of. And a fourth state exists on purpose: nothing to forecast, for someone with no open work — the floor is not the reason there, and using the floor's words would be a different lie.
  8. An empty chart is drawn, never left blank. Its axes, its scale and a sentence on its own plot area, on a faint dashed ground. A blank rectangle is indistinguishable from a chart that failed to render, and this screen has whole teams whose charts are empty.
  9. The forecast column carries a faint hatched ground so that 27 refusals in 31 rows read as one measured region instead of 27 holes.
  10. Collection coverage sits in the person cell, as "N repos observed · denominator unknown", and the non-comparability sentence sits beside the table. The platform can name the repositories it observed for a person and cannot name the ones it did not; until it can, the wording says so rather than implying a ratio — see open question 5.
  11. The screen reports the observation, never the conclusion. SQUAD PINK's band says "nothing opened or closed in eight weeks" and lists the readings that produce that table — a stalled board, a blocked dependency, five people working elsewhere — without choosing one. Choosing one would be analysis the collection does not support.

Open — each one is the maintainer's to settle

  1. How many people open at once? FR-087 proposes three, and says the number is proposed and not measured. Two also keeps the query ceiling (SC-031) comfortable rather than tight.
  2. Throughput and delivered are the same numbers. Chart 3 draws the bars of chart 2 again, read as a pace instead of against what was opened. Both were asked for by name. Recommended: (a) for the first version — the redundancy is visible and named, and dropping a chart the maintainer asked for by name is not the design's call.
  3. The two derivations this table introduces need a name in the knowledge base before the code. The change across the window (first sample against last) and weeks with a close (periods with a non-zero value) are readings of declared measures, not new measures — but nothing reaches the screen without a declaration (constitution, principle IV). (a) declare both as readings on flow.open_work.cumulative and flow.throughput.rate; (b) drop both columns and leave the table with three numbers. Recommended: (a) — without them the table shows a stock with no direction and a total with no regularity, and FR-089 requires the series and not a lone value.
  4. Does the "promised" caveat at the top of the section count as "beside" the word? The reading of FR-063 that accepts this screen and the one that rejects it.   5. Coverage without a denominator. FR-111 wants collection coverage beside the numbers; repositories_of_person/2 lists what was observed and nothing counts what was not.   6. Does a person see their own row when they do not reach the team? Inherited from the spec, and untouched by this design: pode_ver_equipe/3 has four paths and none of them is "it is about me".   7. Whose page carries the four charts in the long run? /people/:id already holds closings by month, open-age buckets and lead time. If it grows these four, this tab keeps only the table and the comparison of two — which may be the right shape. (a) as designed here; (b) the four charts move to the person's page and this tab links to them; (c) both, and the two screens are kept in step by hand. Recommended: (a) now, (b) later — but not (c), which is two screens drifting apart by construction.

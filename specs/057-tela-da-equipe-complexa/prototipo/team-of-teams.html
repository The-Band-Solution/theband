where “still open” is

It is not a third line — it is the distance between the two, hatched because it is derived and not observed. Reading the gap is the whole chart: it widens when the team opens faster than it closes, and narrows when it catches up. At the end of the period it is 19.

A third line would draw the same fact twice, and invite someone to read it against a baseline it does not have.

what this is not

There is no committed scope here, so this does not answer will the sprint finish. The scope is simply everything opened so far. What it answers is narrower and real: is the team closing as fast as it opens.

And closed is the issue being closed at the source — an act of the tool, not a declared end criterion. A task abandoned and one finished look the same here.

what the two runs say together

The team can clear what is open in three to four weeks. It still ends the quarter with more open work than it has today — a median of 48 against 19 now.

Both are true because they answer different questions. Work arrives at 9.5 a week and leaves at 7.1. The gap is small enough to miss in any single week and large enough to decide the quarter, which is exactly the kind of thing a forecast is for and a status meeting is not.

Only 2 of the 10 000 runs cleared the backlog with arrivals included. That is the finding, and no amount of working harder inside the current rates changes it.

before anyone plans against this

Eight weeks is a thin history, and it assumes next quarter resembles last — same people, same kind of work. A team that just lost a member has no such history. Read week 4 as the 85% line from what we have seen, never as a date that was promised.
